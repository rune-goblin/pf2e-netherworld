# Through the Glass

### The Pursuit of Lord Veyrin — A Shadow Plane Encounter Arc

For a party of four at 14th level.

---

## Overview

The strigoi Lord Veyrin flees into the Shadow Plane, and the party follows through a tall looking-glass. Beyond it lies a drained, lightless echo of the world. Somewhere ahead, a second mirror opens onto the frozen palace of Natalia, the Queen of Shadows; Veyrin's coffin lies safely stowed somewhere in this demiplane.

The arc runs in three scenes:

1. **The Shattered Gallery** — the party's own reflections drag them through and attack. *(Combat; the opener.)*
2. **The Threshold of Pain** — a fiend offers passage for a price. *(Roleplay; optional, cut for time.)*
3. **The Dark Mirror** — the party fights Veyrin and chooses whether to shatter the gateway. *(Combat; the finale.)*

The arc turns on one decision in the final room: break the mirror and fight Veyrin on fair footing but lose the planar shortcut, or leave it whole to keep the fast road to the Queen of Shadows and fight him at full strength on his home turf.

---

## Running the Shadow Plane

A few persistent rules give the place its texture. Establish them early; they pay off in the finale.

- **The light is dimmed (the key rule).** Every light source works one step lower here: bright light is only dim, dim light is darkness, and magical light is halved in radius and brightness. In practice the party fights with **concealment against their own targets** — a DC 5 flat check to hit anything they're picking out by sight — while creatures native to the dark, Veyrin included, see them perfectly. The final mirror lifts this condition.
- **Reflections lie.** Polished surfaces show things that aren't there, or the party doing what they never did. Now and then the reflection is the real threat and the original is the decoy.
- **Distance lies.** The plane compresses. Veyrin takes shortcuts the party can't match without a guide or hard checks — the in-fiction reason the chase keeps slipping and they keep arriving *after* him.
- **The dark is hungry.** Standing in unlit areas leeches warmth, color, and eventually memory. Reward movement; punish hiding in the black.

---

## The Crossing

As each PC reaches for the mirror, their own reflection seizes their hand and hauls them through. Resolve a **Will save (DC 32)** as they pass, then drop them straight into the Shattered Gallery and roll initiative.

| Degree | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Critical success** | None. They come through clear-eyed. |
| **Success** | Frightened 1. |
| **Failure** | Frightened 2 and stunned 1. |
| **Critical failure** | Frightened 2 and stunned 2. |

## Encounter 1: The Shattered Gallery

The reflections emerge here because this is where the party emerges. The opener.

### Area description (for mapping)

*A long hall of black marble, polished to a dark mirror-sheen and veined with pale cracks like frost spreading across glass. Tall mirrors in tarnished silver line both long walls; on the near wall one stands taller than the rest, its face boiling with cold white lightning — the glass the party was dragged through. At the hall's heart a great ring is graven into the floor, and within it a slow vortex of shadow turns: faces rise and dissolve in the smoke — a girl, a young woman, a crowned queen, a withered thing of frost and hunger — the same face at every age, each one drawn down and devoured as the dark drinks the Queen of Shadows' life across all her years. Scattered blades lie where they fell. Far across the hall, a broad stone archway waits, leading deeper toward the Threshold.*

A long mirror hall, roughly **60 ft by 30 ft** (12 × 6 squares), entered at one short end with the exit archway at the far end. The gallery is **intact and pristine — no broken glass anywhere.** The "shattering" is what the *reflections* do when they die, not the state of the room.

- **Wall mirrors.** Both long walls are lined floor-to-ceiling with tall, unbroken mirrors in tarnished silver frames, a dozen or so per side. The reflections step out of these. Two or three are **"deep" portal-mirrors** that glow faintly: step into one and drop out of another across the hall — a move option for both sides that sells "distance lies."
- **The mirror grove (key feature).** Rows of free-standing cheval mirrors and mirror-faced pillars stud the floor, breaking the hall into lanes and corridors. They are **cover and line-of-sight blockers**, and the heart of the tactics here: duck behind one to break line of sight to your reflection and it has nothing to echo.
- **The shadow vortex (central feature).** A great ring is graven into the marble at the hall's center, and within it a slow whirlpool of shadow turns — showing the Queen of Shadows at every age of her life, girl to crowned queen to frost-hungry thing, each image rising and being pulled under and consumed, as though the dark were drinking her life essence across all of time. A focal point and contested ground the doubles fight to hold; standing on the ring is its own slow unease (flavor — give it a mechanical bite only if you want one).
- **Floor.** Black marble polished to a weak mirror-sheen, throwing back a faint upside-down echo of whoever stands on it. Tattered carpet runners and toppled velvet ropes tangle a few squares into **minor difficult terrain** (no glass).
- **Light.** Dim throughout (the plane's gloom); no natural light source.
- **Entry and exit.** The arrival mirror on the near wall; a broad stone archway at the far end, leading deeper toward the Threshold.

### The mirrors (two kinds)

Not all the glass here is equal, and the difference is a tactic.

**The way-mirror (the arrival mirror).** The mirror the party came through is a true portal, as tough as the great Glass in the finale — here, **Hardness 14, ~120 HP.** Tough on purpose, but not safe. Destroy it and the party loses the road back, **trapped on this side** and forced to press forward the long way. A careless area effect can strand them, and the reflections — or anything else lurking here — may try to break it deliberately to cut off the retreat. Flag the risk and let the players sweat it.

**The grove mirrors (the free-standing glass).** Every cheval mirror and mirror-pillar in the grove is fragile, linked glass: **no Hardness, 15 HP each.** They are cover and sight-line blockers, but also a weapon. The reflections are bound to the shared surface of the glass, so **when a grove mirror shatters, every reflection takes 15 damage** (no save). Smashing one pane is a small area attack on the whole pack rather than a single Strike on one double. (The tall wall mirrors are sturdier scenery the doubles emerge from — don't worry about smashing those; the glowing portal-pairs are movement features, not targets.)

The catch keeps it honest: the grove is also the party's cover and the source of the break-line-of-sight trick, and the supply is finite. Spend the panes to soften every double at once, or keep them up for positioning — not both. *Tuning:* 15 per pane against doubles of ~120 HP makes it strong but fair (about a third of a double's health to smash the whole grove), paid in actions and lost cover. Slide to 10 or 20 to taste, or add a basic Reflex save if you want the doubles to shrug it off sometimes.

### The reflections

**Recommended method — mirror the players' own sheets.** Each reflection uses its original PC's statistics, with these dials so it stays a fight and not a slog:

- **Reduced HP** — they're glass. Roughly half the PC's HP.
- **Trimmed casting** — at-will cantrips and focus options plus one or two of the PC's prepared spells, not the full list.
- **No death saves** — at 0 HP they shatter instantly (see below).

A full-power copy of the whole party lands around an **Extreme**-threat fight — a lot for an opener stacked on top of the crossing conditions. The dials above bring it to a hard-but-fair **Severe**.

**Fallback method — generic Shadow Doubles (Creature 12).** If you'd rather not run four PC sheets, stamp out the block below, reskinned per PC. Four of them is a **Moderate** encounter; use Creature 13 versions for **Severe**.

> **SHADOW DOUBLE — CREATURE 12**
> *Medium, shadow, undead* (built to GM Core level-12 benchmarks; verify in your tools)
> **Perception** +22; darkvision
> **AC** 34; **Fort** +21, **Ref** +24, **Will** +18
> **HP** 150 (deliberately low — fragile glass)
> **Speed** 25 ft
> **Mirrored Strike** (melee, agile or not per the PC it copies) +26, **Damage** 2d10+13 of the original's signature type (~24)
> **Spellcasting / abilities:** approximate the original's signature trick at DC 31.
> **Shatter** (death): when reduced to 0 HP, the double bursts. Adjacent creatures take 3d6 slashing (basic Reflex DC 31).

### The Mirror Echo

On its turn, a reflection replays whatever its original did **the previous round**, as best it can — always a one-round lag, regardless of initiative order.

- **Round 1:** nothing to copy yet, so the reflections have one round to act with autonomy.
- **Round 2+:** the echo kicks in; each reflection repeats its original's prior-round actions.
- **If it can't replicate something**, the double makes a basic Strike or the nearest equivalent. GM's call.

Two things make this sing at the table:

- **Players can feed the echo.** Once they spot the lag, they can spend a harmless round — Stride in a circle, Recall Knowledge — knowing the double will waste its next turn copying it. Reward the cleverness.
- **Unmirrorable acts leave it exposed.** If a PC does something the double genuinely can't follow — breaks line of sight to it, or shatters the pane it emerged from — that double is **stunned 1 until the end of its next turn**, with nothing to echo.

## Encounter 2: The Threshold of Pain *(optional)*

A social breather between the two fights. Purely additive: skipping it just means the finale runs at standard difficulty. Cut it for time without consequence.

### Area description (for mapping)

*The way out of the gallery opens onto nothing — a lightless gulf with no floor and no far wall the eye can find, falling away forever in every direction. Out in the black hang islands of fused stone and chain: great discs swaying on links that climb into the dark overhead, strung together by bridges barely wide enough to walk single-file. Cold blue lanterns burn along the chains, the only light, and dim at that. One disc near the middle has cracked and half-fallen, bleeding rubble into the gulf. Far across the spans, set in a sheer wall that runs off into the dark without end, a great door woven from blades and chains waits — and before it, on a throne knotted from fused chain, something patient is already watching you cross.*

A scatter of **suspended platforms**, not one floor: circular discs of fused stone and iron chain hanging in a lightless gulf with no bottom and no bounds, linked by narrow bridges. The whole place sways faintly on chains that climb into the void-black overhead.

- **Platforms and bridges.** Three or four discs, each ~20–30 ft across, joined single-file by **narrow chain-and-stone bridges** — room to talk and spread out, but treacherous in a fight. The **central disc is cracked and half-collapsed**, its broken edge unstable footing that sheds rubble into the gulf.
- **Chains everywhere** — draped and crossing, descending from the void-black above. Some end in cruel hooks; several bear swaying **lanterns of cold blue light**, the only illumination, and dim at that.
- The **Shaper of Ecstasy** presides at the far disc before the blade-door, on a throne knotted from fused chain, his two **Sacristans** flanking him; he does not rise.
- **The edges fall away into the dark — on every side.** There is no safe perimeter out here. Going over any edge is a long drop into shadow — a severe fall, or a one-way trip into deeper void, at your discretion.

### The brokers

One presence holds the threshold, and two attend him. He names himself the **Shaper of Ecstasy** — grandly, as though the cutting were a kindness — though it is simpler to call him the **Flesh Shaper**: a unique Interlocutor velstrac (**Creature 16**), waiting on a throne knotted from fused chain, flanked by **two Sacristans (Creature 10 velstracs)**, the patient hands that do his cutting. *The Shaper of Ecstasy ships prebuilt in this module's `pf2e-netherworld.bestiary` compendium (a Creature 16 Interlocutor); drag the two Sacristans (Creature 10) in from Pathfinder Monster Core 2.* He does not rise. A fight isn't the point — draw steel and the threshold recedes into the chains and the dark — but the Shaper and his Sacristans are real muscle if the table forces it (see Refusal). He has all the time the dark can hold, and he already knows why the party has come.

### The deal

The threshold strikes **one bargain** — one gift, paid in full by a **single person**. Not a little from each: everything from one. What makes it a real choice rather than flavor is that the gifts are concrete, fight-changing edges against the wall the party is about to hit. They get **one**, so the table's decision is sharp: *which edge matters most, and who carries the cost?*

Lay all three out and let them weigh it (use whichever fit your group):

**The Sacrament of Pain — make Veyrin killable.** The Shaper of Ecstasy works a hook of cold iron into the bearer and names it a sacrament. In the finale, the bearer's Strikes **ignore Veyrin's physical resistances, and once they've drawn his blood his fast healing never switches back on** — a regenerating vampire that stops regenerating. Roughly halves the hardest fight in the arc.

The hook also *answers*: once per day the bearer can lash out with it as a **chain Strike** modeled on the velstrac evangelist's — a melee one-action Strike (disarm, magical, reach, trip, unholy) dealing **2d8+7 piercing plus 1d6 persistent bleed**, the bearer adding their Strength as usual. On a **critical hit** the target is impaled and anchored: it becomes **grabbed** by the chain and **cannot recover from persistent bleed** until it Escapes against the bearer's class/Athletics DC.

*Cost:* the wound never closes, and it reopens in battle. **The moment the bearer rolls initiative, the hook tears and they take persistent bleed 2d6** (flat DC 15 to end, recovering as normal). And the chain exacts its own toll — **each time the bearer makes the chain Strike they take another persistent bleed 2d6.** The cold iron cuts both the vampire and the one who wields it; the hook stays in until something profound cuts it out, and the sacrament is theirs to keep.

*In Foundry this ships as @UUID-linked compendium items: **The Sacrament of Pain** (`pf2e-netherworld.items`) grants the chain Strike, **Effect: The Wound Reopens** (`.effects`) is dropped on the bearer at initiative to inflict the 2d6 bleed, and **Effect: Impaling Chain** (`.effects`) is the crit rider applied to the target.*

**The Shroud of the Nameless — a ward against the killing cold.** The Shaper of Ecstasy draws the threshold's cold light into the bearer, and a thread of it never leaves them: **permanent resistance 10 to cold**, which blunts the Queen of Shadows' rime in the finale (the 6d8 each round becomes far more survivable) and any cold that comes after. They also keep a sliver of the dark's voice — **once per day they can cast *Wave of Despair*** (rank 5; 30-ft cone, Will save) as an innate spell, at a spell DC appropriate to their level. *(Want the original party-wide ward instead? Have the Flesh Shaper wrap the whole party so the rime can't touch any of them and they ignore the difficult terrain, and treat the cold-resistance item as the bearer's lasting keepsake.)*
*Cost:* the bearer gives their **name** to the dark. The world half-forgets them — allies grope for what to call them, and they take a permanent **−2 status penalty to Diplomacy** — until they win it back.

**Shadow's Clarity — he can't hide, he can't run.** A velstrac eye grafted in place of the bearer's, and it is **permanent**: the bearer gains **greater darkvision** and forever **ignores the concealed condition from darkness, dim light, and shadow** — no creature gains cover-of-sight against them in the gloom, in this fight or any after. In the finale the bearer sees Veyrin even when he melts into shadow and can point the party straight at him, so **he can never use the gloom to hide from the one who carries the eye** — and once in the fight the bearer can call the chains to **seize and hold him** (a powerful immobilize), denying him the high ground or escape through the glass.
*Cost:* the bearer's own eye, and their **reflection** — no glass or water ever shows them again (nothing can scry or mirror-trap them, but the things in shadow now know them on sight). And the eye recoils from the day: **while in bright light or natural daylight the bearer is dazzled** — the dark is theirs, but the instant the Glass shatters and the light returns, and ever after in sunlight, the gift turns against them.

*In Foundry, **Shroud of the Nameless** and **Shadow's Clarity** ship as @UUID-linked equipment in `pf2e-netherworld.items`. The Shroud auto-applies the **cold resistance 10** and the **−2 status penalty to Diplomacy** (the* Wave of Despair *cast is tracked by hand); Shadow's Clarity grants **greater darkvision** (the dazzled-in-light cost is the standard Dazzled condition, applied by the GM). The remaining flavor costs — the lost name, the lost eye and reflection — and the finale chain-seize are GM-adjudicated.*

Each gift is real enough to want; each price actually hurts; only one of them pays. **Map the cost to whoever volunteers** — Harrow speaking a buried truth as his name is taken, Aldric surrendering the reflection, Gene the name his songs are sung under — and let it be a badge they chose, not a punishment dropped on them. The king stepping forward to bear it for his people lands as spotlight, not feel-bad.

**Veyrin paid the same shape of toll — a single person, too.** Not himself: a victim he carried into the dark and left on the chains. The Shaper of Ecstasy will say so, pleasantly, if asked.

**Refuse, and nothing stops them.** The blade-door opens, the toll goes unpaid, and they walk on having spent only time — which, in a pursuit, was the one thing they couldn't spare. *If you want refusal to carry teeth, the threshold has muscle: the Shaper of Ecstasy (Creature 16) and his two Sacristans (Creature 10) are already about a **Moderate** fight on their own (~80 XP for four PCs). Add a Precentor warden (Creature 16) or two more Sacristans to push a walk-away to **Severe** (~120 XP). Use it only if the table forces the issue.*

## Encounter 3: The Dark Mirror

The finale: the dark reflection of Ash Manor's great hall — inverted, decayed, drained of color — where Veyrin makes his stand before the gateway home.

### Area description (for mapping)

*The blade-door opens on the drowned echo of a hall the party may know — Ash Manor's great hall, inverted and decayed, every surface furred with creeping frost. Tattered banners hang stiff with rime; an overturned banquet table splits the floor; the flagstones are sheeted in cracked, blue-white ice that groans underfoot. A balcony runs the long walls and wraps to the far end, where a towering mirror is set high in a frame of black ice — and through it, clear as a window, lies the frozen palace of the Queen of Shadows, she herself watching down without expression. Below the glass, on a low dais, rests a coffin: Veyrin's, where he withdraws to drink the dark and rise stronger.*

A grand hall, roughly **50 ft by 40 ft** (10 × 8 squares), with the great mirror dominating the far short wall.

- **The Glass to the Ice Palace.** A towering mirror set **high on the far wall, ~15 ft up**, in a frame of black ice — out of reach from the floor. Through it, clear as a window, lies the frozen palace of the Queen of Shadows; she stands within, watching down from that height, impassive. This is the smashable objective (full block below).
- **The Queen's rime.** While the mirror stands, frost creeps across floor and walls — the Queen of Shadows' power bleeding through the glass (hazard below). The footing is slick, rimed ice, and the hall stays frozen until the mirror cracks.
- **Central feature.** A long banquet table runs down the middle, overturned and frost-rimed — **standard-to-greater cover**, and a barrier that splits the room.
- **Upper gallery.** A balcony runs along one long wall about 15 ft up and **wraps around the corner to the far wall, passing right beside the mirror** — so the gallery is the only melee approach to the glass. A half-collapsed stair reaches it (an Athletics check, or treat the stair as difficult terrain). This is the high ground Veyrin wants to hold.
- **Veyrin's coffin.** A coffin on a low dais on the floor directly beneath the mirror — the seat of his power here, where he retreats to rest and regain his strength. (Swap in a throne if you prefer, but the coffin sells the strigoi at rest.)
- **Light.** Dim (the plane's gloom) until the mirror breaks.
- **Exits.** Back the way they came, toward the gallery; and the mirror itself, the gateway onward — *only while it remains intact.*

### The Queen's Rime (while the mirror stands)

The Queen of Shadows channels her power through the glass, sheathing the hall in creeping frost. At the **start of each turn**, a creature in the rime takes **6d8 cold damage, basic Fortitude save (DC 38)** — and on a failure is also **clumsy 1** (clumsy 2 on a critical failure) with a **−10-foot status penalty to Speeds** while clumsy. The whole floor is **difficult terrain** (slick, rimed ice). The plane's gloom already blankets the room in dim light, so the frost adds no concealment.

- **Modeled on Frozen Fog.** This is a trimmed, room-wide *Frozen Fog* (the rank-6 spell): cold, basic Fort, clumsy-and-slow on a failure. A basic save still leaves a real clock at this level — the party can ride a round or two, but the tax compounds, and they control the off-switch: break the mirror, or leave the hall. Bump to 8d8 or raise the DC if you want it to bite harder.
- **Veyrin is untouched — no immunity required.** The rime is the Queen's and under her control, so it simply spares her ally, the way any caster excludes allies from an area effect. You don't touch his stat block, and the party's own cold attacks still land on him normally — which matters if Aldric throws frost, where blanket cold immunity would feel bad. *Optional spice:* let the rime actively *bolster* him — fast healing or temporary HP while it rages — so breaking the mirror also chokes off his sustain.
- **Cold, not void.** Void would auto-justify his immunity (undead are healed by void, not harmed), but it's off-theme for the Queen of Shadows — and ice is cooler. Keep it cold and spare him by fiat.
- **Ends at Broken.** The instant the mirror reaches Broken, the channel is cut and the rime melts away — no more cold, no more difficult terrain.

*In Foundry this ships as the hazard below: the Glass emits the rime as an **aura** that grants a* Frozen Fog*-style effect to everything caught in it (the module's `pf2e-netherworld.hazards` and `.effects` compendiums). The recurring 6d8 + basic save is run from the hazard's routine each round.*

### Veyrin

No full block — use whatever you have for the strigoi (≈ level 16). What matters here is how he plays the room:

- **He fights for the gloom.** As long as the light stays dimmed, he has concealment to exploit, shadows to Hide in, and a party fighting at a visibility disadvantage. He breaks line of sight, vanishes, and re-enters off-guard. Lean into the strigoi's bestial side — in his own domain the noble facade is gone.
- **He holds the high ground.** The mirror is his road home and his edge, mounted where the floor can't reach it. He guards the gallery approach and the stair, throws anyone who climbs back down, and baits the party away from the wall — every round they can't get a melee weapon to the glass is a round it stays whole.
- **The Queen of Shadows watches.** She does not intervene directly, but her presence behind the glass is the campaign's next hook, standing right there in frame.

### The Mirror (the central choice)

> **THE GLASS TO THE ICE PALACE** — **Complex Hazard 14** *(object)*
> **Stealth** +28 for initiative; 0 to notice the Glass. **AC** 33; **Fort** +28, **Ref** +22. **Immunities** object immunities.
> **Hardness** 14 — halved to **7** against adamantine (Harrow's standard-grade sword and the needle darts, assuming the darts read as adamantine). Non-adamantine attacks face the full 14 and chip slowly.
> ⚠ *Adamantine only halves a Hardness it can match: a **standard-grade** adamantine weapon is Hardness 14, **high-grade** 17. Keep the Glass's Hardness **≤ 14** or Harrow's standard-grade blade loses the halving and grinds at full Hardness. To make the Glass tougher, raise **HP/BT, not Hardness**.*
> **HP** 150; **Broken Threshold** 75
> **Position:** mounted ~15 ft up the far wall, out of floor melee reach. **Ranged** adamantine (the needle darts) hits it from the floor. **Melee** (Harrow's sword) needs the gallery that wraps to it — up the half-collapsed stair — or a Climb up the frost-slick wall (Athletics, off-guard while climbing).
> **Disable:** Thievery DC 38 (master) to craze the surface, or *dispel magic* (7th rank, counteract DC 36) to sever the Queen's channel — or simply destroy it.
> *Built on the* Distortion Mirror *(Hazard 14, Pathfinder Dark Archives) stat conventions. Optional homebrew: rule it **immune to non-adamantine damage** for a true adamantine-keyed artifact rather than merely resistant.*

**At Broken (75 HP):** the glass spiderwebs and the Queen's channel is severed — **the rime melts at once.** No more cold, no more difficult terrain — the single biggest swing in the fight, and a clear telegraph that they're getting somewhere.

**At 0 HP (shattered):** the −1 light-level condition is **removed entirely.** Light works normally, the party can illuminate the room, and Veyrin loses both the party's concealment penalty *and* his ability to hide in shadow — a vampire in a well-lit room with nowhere to vanish. The Queen of Shadows' reach is severed for good, and **the gateway closes permanently.**

**Important:** breaking the mirror never exposes Veyrin to sunlight. His sunlight vulnerability keys on *natural* sunlight specifically; a restored light level — even the party's own magical light — does not trigger it. The mirror is a **tactics dial, not a kill switch.**

**Make the break a commitment.** The elevation is what gives the choice teeth. At effective Hardness 7 the darts can grind it down from the floor, but they're the slow tool — several rounds of a caster ignoring the vampire. The fast route is Harrow's adamantine sword, which means climbing to the gallery and fighting for the high ground Veyrin most wants to hold, defending a chokepoint. Even there, 150 HP is roughly **four rounds** of diverted effort with the adamantine blade (about **four landed hits to Broken, seven to shatter**; closer to seven and fourteen with a non-adamantine weapon), and every one is a round Veyrin acts unanswered. Scale the HP to make the trade sharper or softer.

---

## Aftermath: the fork

The choice in that room forks the next leg of the campaign:

- **They break the mirror.** They win an easier fight on fair footing, but hand the Queen of Shadows weeks of warning while they make the long overland journey to her palace across the Material Plane. She gets time to prepare.
- **They leave it whole.** They take Veyrin at full strength on his own turf, but step straight through the glass into the Queen of Shadows' domain for a fast strike before she's ready.

Either branch is real content, and the players chose it.

## Quick reference

**Crossing (Will DC 32):** crit success — none; success — frightened 1; failure — frightened 2 + stunned 1; crit failure — frightened 2 + stunned 2. No surprise round; reflections roll Stealth/Deception for initiative; un-acted PCs are off-guard to their reflection.

**Encounter budget (4 PCs, level 14):** Moderate 80 XP · Severe 120 · Extreme 160. Reflections at full sheets ≈ Extreme; trimmed ≈ Severe; four Creature 12 doubles ≈ Moderate.

**Gallery mirrors:** arrival way-mirror = Hardness 14, ~120 HP — break it and the party is trapped on this side. Grove mirrors = no Hardness, 15 HP; shattering one deals 15 (no save) to every reflection, but costs cover and the line-of-sight trick.

**Mirror (Complex Hazard 14):** AC 33, Fort +28 / Ref +22, object immunities. Hardness 14 (7 vs. adamantine — keep Hardness ≤14 or adamantine stops halving), HP 150, BT 75. Broken = Queen's rime ends. Shattered = light normal, Veyrin loses concealment/hiding, gateway closed, no sunlight effect.

**The Queen's rime (while mirror whole):** 6d8 cold, **basic Fort DC 38** (clumsy 1 + −10-ft Speed on a fail; clumsy 2 on a crit fail), each turn, to all but Veyrin; floor is difficult terrain; no concealment (dim light covers that). Modeled on *Frozen Fog*. Ends the instant the mirror hits Broken.
